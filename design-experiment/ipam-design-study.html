IPAM as an operational record, not a generic dashboard.

Three deliberately different interface directions for scoped inventory, evidence-aware reconciliation, capacity, and approval work. The examples use invented data only. They are visual proposals, not new application behavior.

What the interface must make obvious

The present product already models an important distinction: an inventory record is not live evidence, a saved reconciliation run is not a current workflow state, and incomplete evidence is not a clean result. These concepts keep that hierarchy visible before they optimize surface polish.

Non-negotiable visual rules
  • Scope is persistent, never implied by a loose filter.
  • Evidence states use plain words plus color, not color alone.
  • Actions sit beside the record they affect, while saved evidence stays immutable.
  • Capacity is a table-and-explanation problem before it is a chart problem.

Recommendation: start with Atlas.

It is the best match for the existing app's broad but bounded workflow: inventory browsing, saved evidence, capacity inspection, schedule state, corrections, and approvals. It earns its personality through typography, hierarchy, and the chain-of-custody treatment of evidence—not gradients, oversized KPIs, or decorative charts.

What to borrow from the other directions
  • From Console: the evidence-source matrix and dense triage ordering.
  • From Ledger: a prose-first exception case summary and clear current-vs-saved labels.
  • Do not combine all three visual languages in one screen.
Prototype only. All names, identifiers, quantities, and timestamps shown above are synthetic. Buttons and controls are illustrative; they do not call the application API.
